CarTron: Autonomous Mobile Manipulation for Shopping-Cart Management

A task-oriented autonomous robotic platform that combines mobile navigation, environmental perception, and robotic-arm manipulation to perform physical tasks in large parking environments, demonstrated through autonomous shopping-cart pushing and pulling.

Project Overview

CarTron investigates how an autonomous mobile robot equipped with a robotic manipulator can move beyond navigation-only capabilities and perform useful physical tasks in complex outdoor environments.

The platform integrates autonomous navigation, environmental perception, localization, mapping, obstacle avoidance, and robotic-arm manipulation into a unified mobile-manipulation system. Shopping-cart management was selected as a representative use case because it requires the robot not only to identify and navigate toward a target, but also to physically interact with and transport objects.

In our demonstration, the robot autonomously navigates through a parking environment, approaches shopping carts, positions itself for manipulation, and uses the onboard robotic arm to push and pull carts. The project serves as a practical demonstration of combining autonomous mobility and manipulation for repetitive outdoor service tasks.

Motivation: Automating Repetitive Outdoor Tasks

Large retail stores and parking facilities depend on repetitive manual operations such as locating, collecting, and returning shopping carts. These tasks require continuous human labor across large outdoor areas and may become more difficult during periods of high demand or challenging weather conditions.

CarTron explores whether autonomous robotic systems can complement these operations by combining mobility, environmental awareness, and physical manipulation within the same robotic platform.

Large parking facilities and changing workforce demands motivate automation of repetitive outdoor retail operations.

Shopping-cart collection remains a labor-intensive activity involving scattered carts, large parking areas, traffic interaction, and changing environmental conditions.

Key Contributions

Mobile Manipulation

Integration of an autonomous mobile base and robotic manipulator enables the platform to navigate to a target and physically interact with it.

Autonomous Navigation

LiDAR, camera, and IMU sensing support localization, mapping, obstacle avoidance, and navigation through parking environments.

Object Localization

Environmental perception is used to identify and spatially localize shopping carts before navigation and manipulation.

Cart Push and Pull

The robotic arm is used to establish physical interaction with carts and demonstrate both pushing and pulling operations.

Task-Level Autonomy

Navigation and manipulation are coordinated as part of an end-to-end workflow rather than treated as independent robotic functions.

Autonomous Cart-Management Workflow

The CarTron concept connects store-level monitoring with autonomous navigation and physical manipulation through a multi-stage operational workflow.

1

Monitor

Detect a cart shortage or identify the need for cart collection within the parking environment.

2

Trigger

Generate a deployment request and activate the robotic collection task.

3

Locate

Perception sensors identify and spatially localize shopping carts distributed across the parking area.

4

Navigate

The AMR autonomously travels toward the selected cart while avoiding cars, pedestrians, and static obstacles.

5

Manipulate

The robot positions its manipulator and interacts with the cart for pushing, pulling, or collection.

6

Return

The cart is transported toward a designated collection or store-entry location.

Proposed workflow from monitoring and cart localization to robot deployment, autonomous navigation, cart manipulation, and return.

System Architecture

CarTron is organized as a modular robotic system combining perception, navigation, manipulation, and task-level decision making.

The onboard sensing configuration includes LiDAR, cameras, and IMU measurements. These sensors provide environmental information for cart detection, localization, mapping, and obstacle-aware navigation. A robotic manipulator mounted on the mobile platform provides the physical interaction capability required for cart handling.

Detection & Trigger

External monitoring or store-level sensing can identify cart demand and trigger the robotic task.

Environment Perception

LiDAR and camera sensing are used to perceive the parking environment and identify target carts.

Mapping & Planning

Environmental maps and obstacle information are used to plan paths toward target carts while avoiding dynamic and static obstacles.

Autonomous Navigation

The mobile robot executes the planned trajectory and continuously reacts to vehicles, pedestrians, and other environmental changes.

Cart Manipulation

The robotic arm aligns with and physically engages the shopping cart for pushing and pulling operations.

Return & Redeployment

After task completion, the system can return the cart to a designated location and prepare for the next task.

Overall system architecture integrating task triggering, environment perception, mapping and planning, autonomous navigation, cart manipulation, and return/deployment.

Autonomous Navigation + Robotic Manipulation

The primary research emphasis of CarTron is the integration of autonomous mobility with physical manipulation. Navigation alone allows a robot to reach a target location; however, many useful service tasks require the robot to interact with objects after arriving.

For the shopping-cart demonstration, the mobile platform first approaches the target cart and establishes a suitable relative position. The manipulator is then used to interact with the cart, enabling the robot to demonstrate both cart pushing and cart pulling.

Demonstrated capability: autonomous navigation to a target cart followed by manipulator-based physical interaction for pushing and pulling the cart within a parking environment.

Connected and Human-Supervised Operation

The overall architecture can also support networked operation through Wi-Fi or cellular connectivity. For situations where fully autonomous execution may be difficult, a teleoperation or human-supervision capability can provide an additional fallback mechanism.

This combination of autonomous execution and human support is particularly useful for edge cases such as blocked pathways, unusual cart configurations, unexpected traffic, or manipulation conditions outside the robot's normal operating envelope.

Beyond Shopping-Cart Collection

Shopping-cart management serves as a representative demonstration of a broader research direction: task-oriented autonomous mobile manipulation. The same underlying architecture can be adapted to other applications where robots must autonomously navigate to a location and physically interact with objects.

Retail Automation

Outdoor material movement, inventory-support operations, and repetitive service tasks.

Industrial Logistics

Movement and manipulation of carts, bins, containers, and other mobile assets.

Campus & Facility Robotics

Autonomous robots that combine navigation with object transport and physical assistance.

Human-Supervised Robotics

Autonomous task execution with remote human assistance for challenging or previously unseen situations.
