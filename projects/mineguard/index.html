Project Overview

SICK-MINE-GUARD is an autonomous robotic platform designed to support safer and more intelligent mining operations through autonomous navigation, 3D environmental mapping, distributed sensing, and real-time data collection.

The project focuses on deploying robotic systems in challenging mining environments where conventional inspection, mapping, and monitoring activities can be labor-intensive, time-consuming, and potentially hazardous for human workers.

The robotic platform integrates LiDAR-based perception, autonomous navigation, communication infrastructure, and intelligent data analysis to create a continuously updated representation of the mining environment. These capabilities can support mine inspection, safety monitoring, evacuation planning, production assessment, and other data-intensive operations.

Why Autonomous Robotics for Mining?

Mining operations involve large, dynamic, and often unstructured environments where workers may be exposed to uneven terrain, unstable geological structures, heavy machinery, limited visibility, and other operational hazards.

Many inspection and monitoring activities still require personnel to physically enter or repeatedly traverse these environments. SICK-MINE-GUARD investigates how autonomous robotic systems can complement human workers by performing repetitive and high-risk data-collection tasks while maintaining detailed digital representations of the mine.

Mining operations require continuous environmental understanding, inspection, localization, safety monitoring, and reliable access to data in difficult and potentially hazardous environments.

The objective is to transform the robot from a simple inspection platform into an intelligent mobile sensing system capable of supporting mine operations and safety.

System Workflow

SICK-MINE-GUARD follows a connected sensing and autonomous data-collection workflow in which the robot continuously observes the mine environment, builds spatial maps, and communicates operational information to mine personnel.

1

Environment Sensing

LiDAR and onboard sensing continuously capture the geometry and surrounding conditions of the mine.

2

Localization & Navigation

The robot estimates its position and autonomously navigates through unstructured mining environments.

3

3D Mapping

Sensor observations are transformed into detailed three-dimensional maps of operational areas.

4

Mesh Communication

A mesh-network architecture supports exchange of robot-generated information across difficult mining environments.

5

Data Analytics

Mapping, environmental, and operational information can be analyzed to support safety and mine-management decisions.

6

Operator Interface

The SICK AppSpace-based application presents processed information to mine personnel for monitoring and decision support.

Real-World Field Deployment

A key objective of the project was to move beyond simulation and laboratory testing by evaluating the robotic system in representative mining and quarry environments.

Field experiments examined autonomous mobility and LiDAR-based mapping in uneven terrain containing standing water, large stone structures, heavy equipment, and other environmental features representative of active mining operations.

Field deployment of the robotic platform in a quarry environment, demonstrating real-world sensing and LiDAR-based spatial data collection under challenging terrain conditions.

3D Mapping and Digital Mine Representation

High-resolution spatial mapping is one of the core capabilities of SICK-MINE-GUARD. LiDAR measurements collected during robot navigation can be combined into a 3D representation of the surrounding environment.

These maps can support structural inspection, localization, volume estimation, route planning, environmental monitoring, and identification of changes in mine geometry over time. Repeated autonomous surveys can therefore provide a continuously updated digital representation of operational areas.

Key concept: transform autonomous mine inspection from a one-time robotic mission into a continuously updated source of spatial, operational, and safety information.

Mesh-Connected Mine Intelligence

Reliable communication is particularly challenging in underground and large-scale mining environments. The project therefore considers a mesh-networked architecture that allows robotic platforms and infrastructure nodes to collaboratively relay information across the mine.

This connectivity enables sensor data, maps, robot status, and safety information to be transferred to an operator interface without requiring a direct communication link between every robot and a central station.

The end-user application developed using SICK AppSpace provides an interface through which operators can access processed information and support operational decision making.

Evacuation Planning

Mine Mapping

Automated 3D surveys provide detailed spatial information for operational planning and infrastructure assessment.

Volume Measurement

Repeated missions provide updated information about mine conditions without requiring continuous manual inspection.

Remote Decision Support

Operational and Business Value

SICK-MINE-GUARD has the potential to create value not only through robotic automation but also through the continuous generation of operational data.

Improved Operational Efficiency

Automating repetitive inspection, mapping, and monitoring activities can reduce the time required to collect information across large mining environments.

Reduced Human Exposure

Robotic sensing can reduce the frequency with which personnel must enter difficult or high-risk areas, supporting safer mine operations.

Reduced Downtime

Faster access to environmental and operational data can help identify issues earlier and support more timely operational decisions.

Data-Driven Mine Operations

Real-time mapping, localization, safety measurements, and environmental information can support improved resource planning and operational management.

Data as a Mining Service

A particularly important aspect of the platform is that the robot generates a continuously growing collection of high-value mining data. This creates opportunities beyond the physical robotic system itself.

3D Mapping Services

Updated digital maps can be provided for mine planning, inspection, infrastructure management, and operational analysis.

Volume Measurement Services

LiDAR-derived 3D measurements can support material volume estimation and change detection.

Safety Data Services

Robot-generated spatial and environmental information can support safety assessment and risk monitoring.

Data Licensing

Mapping and operational datasets could support analytics, digital twins, AI model development, and other mining applications through future licensing or data-service models.

Long-term vision: SICK-MINE-GUARD can evolve from an autonomous inspection robot into a data-driven mining platform that combines robotics, sensing, connectivity, analytics, and digital services.
