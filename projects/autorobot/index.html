AMR: Behaviour model for Indoor Navigation

Ajay Kumar, Tejas Chavan, Rushikesh Zende, Kalyan Sai, Zhi-Li Zhang

Abstract

This work presents a behavior-driven motion modeling framework for autonomous indoor robot navigation in environments shared with pedestrians and static obstacles. Using onboard camera and LiDAR sensing, the robot distinguishes between human and non-human obstacles and adapts its motion profile accordingly to support safer and more context-aware navigation. The proposed system combines visual scene understanding with occupancy-map-based path planning to generate precise local trajectories while accounting for obstacle type and spatial constraints. Experiments were conducted on a fully autonomous mobile robot equipped with LiDAR and camera sensors across a range of challenging indoor scenarios, including narrow passages, static obstructions, and pedestrian-shared spaces. The results demonstrate the feasibility of integrating behavior-aware perception with geometric planning to enable smoother and more adaptive robot motion in dynamic indoor environments.
