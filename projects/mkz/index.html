Level 3 Autonomous Driving

Full-Stack Autonomous Driving

The Autonomous Driving Module for the Lincoln MKZ is a full-stack Level 3 autonomous driving platform developed to support reliable vehicle operation under real-world road conditions.

The system integrates the primary components required for autonomous vehicle operation, including multimodal perception, vehicle localization, behavior and motion planning, and low-level vehicle control. These modules operate together to perceive the surrounding environment, estimate the vehicle pose, determine safe driving behavior, generate feasible trajectories, and execute steering, acceleration, and braking commands.

The complete system was extensively tested through real-vehicle road demonstrations across diverse driving scenarios. The platform successfully satisfied the required regulatory evaluation process and contributed to obtaining an official autonomous-driving license from the Korean government.

Perception

Multi-sensor perception provides information about surrounding vehicles, road structure, obstacles, and other traffic participants.

Localization

LiDAR, camera, GNSS, and inertial sensing support accurate ego-vehicle localization across changing road environments.

Planning

Behavior and trajectory planning determine safe, feasible vehicle motion according to the surrounding traffic environment.

Vehicle Control

Steering, acceleration, and braking controllers execute the generated trajectory on the physical vehicle.

Deployment milestone: the integrated autonomous-driving platform was validated through extensive real-road testing and successfully met regulatory requirements for autonomous-vehicle operation in South Korea.

Autonomous Vehicle with Trailer Attached

Autonomous Trailer Control

The MKZ Trailer Control System extends autonomous-driving capability to a vehicle operating with an attached trailer. Trailer operation introduces additional control complexity because the trailer follows its own articulation dynamics rather than directly following the steering trajectory of the towing vehicle.

The system integrates environment perception, vehicle localization, trajectory generation, and trailer-aware control to support precise maneuvering while accounting for the coupled motion of the vehicle and trailer.

The platform was designed to handle challenging maneuvers including turning, trajectory tracking, and reverse operation. These capabilities are relevant to autonomous logistics, towing, freight transport, and intelligent assistance systems where accurate control of articulated vehicles is required.

Trailer-Aware Modeling

Vehicle and trailer geometry are incorporated into the motion-control framework to account for articulation behavior.

Trajectory Tracking

Control algorithms maintain accurate tracking of the desired path while considering trailer motion.

Reverse Maneuvering

Reverse motion requires coordinated steering control to prevent unstable articulation and excessive trailer angle.

Constrained Maneuvers

The system supports trailer operation in limited-space environments requiring precise positioning and turning.

Research focus: extending autonomous driving from a single rigid vehicle to an articulated vehicle system in which planning and control must explicitly reason about trailer dynamics.

Autonomous Mobility on Demand (AMoD)

Campus Autonomous Mobility

The Mobility-on-Demand pilot at DGIST Campus explores the use of compact electric vehicles for autonomous and remotely supported delivery services within a controlled campus environment.

Users can request vehicle service through a mobile interface, allowing vehicles to be dispatched to different campus locations for transportation of goods, parcels, laboratory materials, and other supplies.

The system combines lightweight autonomous navigation, task dispatch, communication, and teleoperation support to provide reliable mobility while retaining remote human assistance for situations that require intervention.

The pilot demonstrates how autonomous mobility technologies can move beyond passenger driving and support practical campus logistics, facility services, and last-mile delivery.

On-Demand Dispatch

Users request transportation through a mobile interface and the system dispatches an available vehicle.

Autonomous Navigation

The electric vehicle autonomously travels between designated campus locations.

Teleoperation Support

Remote human assistance provides a fallback when the autonomous system encounters difficult or uncertain situations.

Campus Logistics

The platform supports transport of parcels, supplies, goods, and equipment between facilities.

Deployment concept: combining compact electric vehicles, autonomous navigation, mobile service requests, and remote supervision provides a practical model for intelligent campus logistics and last-mile mobility.
