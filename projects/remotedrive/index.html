Abstract

This project explores remote driving and human-in-the-loop Connected and Automated Vehicle (CAV) technologies as a scalable approach to improve mobility, safety, and accessibility in rural areas. It develops a hybrid autonomy framework that combines onboard automated driving capabilities with low-latency remote supervision over heterogeneous communication networks.

The system investigates adaptive transmission of visual, vehicle-state, and multimodal sensor information between the vehicle and a remote operator while maintaining responsive control under changing network conditions. Through system development, network evaluation, multimodal perception, and human-centered studies, the project examines how remote driving can complement onboard autonomy when vehicles encounter situations that require human assistance.

The broader objective is to enable practical deployment of autonomous mobility services in underserved regions, where full autonomy may not yet be available across all operating conditions. Remote assistance can provide an intermediate pathway toward deployment while improving access to mobility for populations such as elderly and mobility-impaired users.

Why Remote Driving?

Fully autonomous vehicles must handle a wide range of long-tail situations, environmental uncertainty, communication limitations, and previously unseen events. Achieving reliable autonomy across every operating condition remains challenging, particularly when autonomous mobility services are deployed outside highly structured environments.

Remote driving introduces a human-supervised autonomy layer that allows autonomous vehicles to request assistance when onboard intelligence is insufficient. Instead of requiring continuous teleoperation, the remote operator can provide supervision, high-level assistance, or direct control only when needed.

Core idea: combine autonomous vehicle intelligence with remote human supervision so that the vehicle can operate autonomously under normal conditions while retaining access to human assistance for difficult or uncertain scenarios.

Bidirectional Remote-Driving Architecture

Remote driving relies on a bidirectional communication loop between the vehicle and the remote operator. Sensor information generated onboard the vehicle is transmitted over the uplink, while operator control commands and assistance information are delivered to the vehicle through the downlink.

Vehicle-to-Operator Uplink

Cameras, vehicle state, and other onboard sensing information provide the remote operator with situational awareness of the driving environment.

Operator-to-Vehicle Downlink

Steering, acceleration, braking, trajectories, or higher-level assistance commands are transmitted back to the vehicle.

Onboard Autonomy

Local perception and vehicle control continue to provide real-time capabilities close to the physical system.

Remote Human Supervision

Human operators provide additional reasoning and control for situations that exceed the autonomous system's operating capability.

Connected Remote-Driving System

The experimental platform separates high-bandwidth visual streaming from time-critical vehicle-control communication. Camera streams are delivered to the operator using WebRTC, while vehicle state and control messages are exchanged using Zenoh over QUIC.

The system continuously observes communication quality, including latency, jitter, and frame loss, allowing the remote-driving stack to reason about changing network conditions and adapt data transmission accordingly.

Adaptive Multimodal Sensor Streaming

Continuously transmitting all raw sensor information is expensive and may increase communication latency during periods of limited bandwidth. The project therefore explores adaptive multimodal communication that prioritizes information according to driving relevance, network conditions, and remote-operator needs.

Camera images and LiDAR point clouds are processed onboard into compact visual and geometric representations. Network-aware adaptation can then modify detection range, regions of interest, transmitted information, or representation fidelity according to latency and available bandwidth.

At the remote side, multimodal features are fused and decoded to reconstruct task-relevant information for the operator, reducing the need to transmit every sensor stream at its maximum fidelity continuously.

Network-aware multimodal communication using onboard image and point-cloud representations, adaptive transmission, feature fusion, and edge-side decoding for remote operator perception.

Key Deployment Challenges

Practical remote-driving systems must address challenges that extend beyond autonomous vehicle perception and control. Communication reliability, security, and representative data are all critical to deploying human-supervised mobility systems at scale.

Low-Latency Communication

Cellular networks can experience congestion, variable bandwidth, jitter, and latency. Remote driving therefore requires communication strategies that adapt sensor streaming and control transmission to network conditions.

Cybersecurity

Network-connected vehicles introduce security concerns involving communication interfaces, vehicle software, sensor integrity, and remote-control channels.

Benchmark Data

Large-scale multimodal datasets are needed to study perception, communication, geolocation, network behavior, and human intervention across diverse operating scenarios.

Major challenges for scalable remote driving include maintaining low-latency communication, securing connected vehicle systems, and developing representative multimodal benchmark datasets.

Human Driver Behavior Dataset

Human-supervised autonomy also requires understanding how experienced drivers perceive and respond to challenging situations. As part of this direction, the project explores collection of human driver behavior data across varied driving scenarios.

Driving scenarios include intersections, pedestrian crossings, turns, overtaking, lane changes, changing traffic conditions, and different environmental conditions. In addition to multimodal vehicle and sensor information, driver-perspective measurements can capture visual attention and decision-making behavior.

Driving Environment

The dataset can include varied road geometries, traffic situations, weather conditions, multimodal sensor streams, and vehicle-state measurements.

Driver Perspective

Human-centered measurements such as eye-gaze behavior, head movement, and focused field of view provide additional insight into how drivers identify and react to critical situations.

Human-driver behavior data collection across varied scenarios, combining multimodal simulator or vehicle data with driver-perspective measurements such as eye gaze, head movement, and focused field of view.

Remote Driving for Rural Mobility

Rural mobility presents a particularly important application for human-supervised autonomous transportation. Low population density, long travel distances, limited public transit, and uneven transportation-service availability can make conventional mobility solutions difficult to scale.

A hybrid autonomous and remote-driving architecture allows a vehicle to complete routine portions of a trip autonomously while requesting remote human assistance for difficult or uncertain situations. This can reduce the need for continuous onboard human operation without requiring the autonomous system to solve every possible driving scenario independently.

Deployment vision: remote driving serves as a transitional layer between conventional human-driven transportation and fully autonomous mobility, enabling autonomous services to be introduced while retaining human support for long-tail and safety-critical situations.

Human-in-the-Loop Autonomous Mobility

The long-term objective is not to replace onboard autonomy with continuous teleoperation. Instead, the project studies how human intelligence and automated driving can complement one another.

Autonomous Operation

Vehicles independently handle routine driving situations within their operational capabilities.

Remote Assistance

Human operators provide high-level guidance when the vehicle encounters ambiguous or unfamiliar situations.

Remote Driving

Direct human control remains available when the situation requires more substantial intervention.

Return to Autonomy

After the difficult situation is resolved, vehicle operation can transition back to onboard autonomy.

Research Directions

The platform provides an experimental foundation for investigating several interconnected problems in connected and autonomous mobility.

Communication-Aware Autonomy

Jointly adapting perception, streaming, and control according to wireless-network conditions.

Adaptive Video Streaming

Prioritizing the most useful views, regions, frame rates, and visual quality for remote driving.

Human–AI Collaboration

Determining when autonomous vehicles should continue independently and when human assistance is necessary.

Long-Tail Driving Scenarios

Studying rare and difficult situations that remain challenging for fully autonomous systems.

Driver Behavior Modeling

Learning how human drivers perceive, interpret, and respond to complex driving environments.

Accessible Mobility

Applying connected and human-supervised autonomy to improve transportation access in underserved communities.
