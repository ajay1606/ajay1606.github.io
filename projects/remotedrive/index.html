Abstract

This project explores remote driving and human-in-the-loop Connected and Automated Vehicle (CAV) technologies as a scalable solution to improve mobility, safety, and accessibility in rural areas. It proposes a hybrid autonomy framework combining onboard automation with low-latency remote supervision using adaptive streaming over heterogeneous communication networks.

Through system design, network evaluation, adaptive multimodal streaming, and human-centered studies, the work investigates how remote driving can complement autonomous vehicle capabilities when vehicles encounter challenging or uncertain situations.

The broader objective is to enable practical deployment of autonomous mobility services in underserved regions while improving transportation accessibility, particularly for elderly and mobility-impaired populations.

Why Remote Driving?

Fully autonomous vehicles must operate across diverse road, weather, traffic, and communication conditions while handling rare and previously unseen situations. Achieving reliable autonomy across every possible condition remains challenging, particularly when autonomous mobility services are deployed outside highly structured environments.

Remote driving introduces a human-supervised autonomy layer that allows autonomous vehicles to request assistance when onboard intelligence is insufficient. Rather than continuously teleoperating the vehicle, a remote operator can provide supervision, high-level assistance, or direct control only when needed.

Core idea: combine autonomous vehicle intelligence with remote human supervision so that the vehicle operates autonomously under normal conditions while retaining access to human assistance for difficult, uncertain, or long-tail scenarios.

Bidirectional Remote-Driving Architecture

Remote driving relies on a bidirectional communication loop between the connected vehicle and the remote operator. Sensor information generated onboard the vehicle is transmitted over the uplink, while operator control commands and assistance information are delivered to the vehicle through the downlink.

Vehicle-to-Operator Uplink

Cameras, vehicle state, and multimodal onboard sensing provide the remote operator with real-time situational awareness.

Operator-to-Vehicle Downlink

Steering, acceleration, braking, trajectory information, or higher-level assistance commands are transmitted back to the vehicle.

Onboard Autonomy

Local perception, planning, and control continue to provide time-critical capabilities close to the physical vehicle.

Remote Human Supervision

Human operators provide additional reasoning and control when the autonomous system reaches the limits of its operating capability.

Connected Remote-Driving System

The experimental platform separates high-bandwidth visual streaming from time-critical vehicle-control communication. Camera streams are delivered to the operator using WebRTC, while vehicle-state information and control messages are communicated through Zenoh over QUIC.

The system continuously observes communication quality, including latency, jitter, frame loss, and available network capacity. This enables the remote-driving stack to adapt streaming and control behavior as communication conditions change.

System Demonstrations

The remote-driving platform was evaluated through multiple demonstrations covering multi-camera video streaming, real-time teleoperation across different driving conditions, and driver-assistance capabilities designed specifically for the remote operator.

Demonstration of real-time teleoperation of the research vehicle across different driving scenarios. The experiment illustrates the complete remote-driving loop, including video feedback, vehicle-state communication, operator control, and execution of remote commands.

Demonstration of Advanced Driver Assistance System (ADAS) functions integrated into the teleoperation interface. The system provides additional perception and driving-context information to improve remote-driver situational awareness and support safer decision making.

Adaptive Multimodal Sensor Streaming

Continuously transmitting all raw sensor information can consume substantial communication bandwidth and increase latency during periods of limited network capacity. The project therefore explores adaptive multimodal communication that prioritizes information according to driving relevance, network conditions, and remote-operator requirements.

Camera images and LiDAR point clouds can be processed onboard into compact visual and geometric representations. Network-aware adaptation can then modify detection range, regions of interest, transmitted information, or representation fidelity according to available bandwidth and latency.

At the remote side, multimodal representations can be fused and decoded to provide task-relevant information to the operator without continuously transmitting every sensor stream at maximum fidelity.

Network-aware multimodal communication using onboard image and point-cloud representations, adaptive transmission, feature fusion, and edge-side decoding for remote operator perception.

Key Deployment Challenges

Practical remote-driving systems must address challenges that extend beyond vehicle perception and control. Communication reliability, cybersecurity, and representative datasets are critical for deploying human-supervised mobility systems at scale.

Low-Latency Communication

Cellular and wireless networks can experience congestion, bandwidth variation, jitter, and latency. Remote driving therefore requires communication-aware adaptation of sensor streaming and control transmission.

Cybersecurity

Network-connected vehicles introduce security concerns involving communication interfaces, software systems, remote-control channels, and sensor integrity.

Benchmark Data

Large-scale multimodal datasets are required to study perception, communication, geolocation, network behavior, and human intervention across diverse driving conditions.

Major challenges for scalable remote driving include maintaining low-latency communication, securing connected vehicle systems, and developing representative multimodal benchmark datasets.

Human Driver Behavior Dataset

Human-supervised autonomy also requires understanding how experienced drivers perceive and respond to challenging situations. The project therefore investigates collection of human driver behavior data across diverse driving scenarios.

Representative scenarios include intersections, pedestrian crossings, turns, overtaking, lane changes, different traffic conditions, and changing environmental conditions. In addition to multimodal vehicle and sensor information, driver-perspective measurements can capture attention and decision-making behavior.

Driving Environment

Data can include varied road geometries, traffic situations, weather conditions, multimodal sensor streams, and vehicle-state measurements.

Driver Perspective

Eye-gaze behavior, head movement, and focused field of view provide insight into how experienced drivers observe, interpret, and respond to critical situations.

Human-driver behavior data collection across varied scenarios, combining multimodal driving data with driver-perspective measurements such as eye gaze, head movement, and focused field of view.

Remote Driving for Rural Mobility

Rural mobility provides an important application for human-supervised autonomous transportation. Long travel distances, lower population density, limited public transportation, and uneven availability of mobility services can make conventional transportation solutions difficult to scale.

A hybrid autonomous and remote-driving architecture allows a vehicle to complete routine portions of a trip autonomously while requesting human assistance for difficult or uncertain situations. This reduces the requirement for continuous onboard human operation without requiring the autonomous vehicle to independently solve every possible driving scenario.

Deployment vision: remote driving can serve as a transitional layer between conventional human-driven transportation and fully autonomous mobility, enabling autonomous services to be introduced while retaining human support for long-tail and safety-critical situations.

Human-in-the-Loop Autonomous Mobility

The long-term objective is not to replace onboard autonomy with continuous teleoperation. Instead, the system explores how autonomous intelligence and remote human expertise can complement one another.

Autonomous Operation

The vehicle independently handles routine driving situations within its operating capability.

Remote Assistance

Human operators provide high-level guidance when the vehicle encounters ambiguous or unfamiliar situations.

Remote Driving

Direct human control is available when a situation requires more substantial intervention.

Return to Autonomy

Once the challenging situation is resolved, vehicle operation can transition back to onboard autonomy.

Research Directions

The platform provides an experimental foundation for studying several interconnected problems in connected, autonomous, and human-supervised mobility.

Communication-Aware Autonomy

Joint adaptation of sensing, communication, perception, and vehicle control according to wireless-network conditions.

Adaptive Video Streaming

Selecting the most useful camera views, regions, frame rates, and visual quality for remote operation.

Human-AI Collaboration

Determining when autonomous vehicles should continue independently and when remote human assistance is necessary.

Long-Tail Driving Scenarios

Investigating rare, difficult, and previously unseen situations that remain challenging for autonomous vehicles.

Driver Behavior Modeling

Learning how human drivers perceive, reason about, and respond to complex driving situations.

Accessible Mobility

Applying connected and human-supervised autonomy to improve transportation access in underserved communities.
